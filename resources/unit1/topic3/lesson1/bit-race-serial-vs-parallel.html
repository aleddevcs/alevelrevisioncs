Bit race: serial vs parallel

Send the same message down a serial cable and a parallel cable at the same time. Change the distance and the clock speeds, then compare what arrives at the other end.

Your three missions

  1. Keep the distance on Inside the computer and both clocks at ×1. Which cable delivers the message first, and roughly how many times faster is it?
    Check your answer

    Parallel wins by about 8 times. It sends all 8 bits of a character together on every clock tick, while serial sends 1 bit per tick. Over short distances this makes parallel faster.

  2. Switch to Network cable and send again. What happened to the parallel message, and why?
    Check your answer

    The parallel message arrives corrupted. Signals on different wires don't travel at exactly the same speed, so the bits of one character drift apart. This is called skew. The longer the cable, the bigger the drift, until bits are read on the wrong clock tick. That is why parallel only works over short distances.

  3. Stay on Network cable and turn crosstalk on. First, try to find a parallel clock speed that delivers the message correctly. Then push the serial clock up. Can serial beat parallel's time with no errors?
    Check your answer

    Parallel can't deliver the message correctly at any clock speed over this distance. Serial can, and if you push its clock high enough it also arrives first. Serial has only one data wire, so there is no skew to go wrong and no neighbouring wires to cause crosstalk. It can therefore be clocked much faster. This is why most modern connections, such as USB, SATA, Ethernet and PCIe, are serial.

Advantages (max 2)

  • Data is transmitted at a higher speed
  • Works well for short-distance communication
  • A set of bits is transferred at the same time

Disadvantages (max 2)

  • More expensive because extra wires are needed
  • Over long distances the wire must be thicker to reduce signal degradation
  • Multiple communication channels are required
  • Crosstalk can occur when many data lines are used at once
